# Klip Copywriting Playbook

Klip's own copywriting system (from the Klip creative team, script studio
and ops desk), adapted to email. Use it together with email_frameworks:
where they disagree, email_frameworks' compliance and length rules win.

## Before writing, know four things
1. **Who**: the person's role and the business type (from the prospect record).
2. **Objective**: start a conversation (cold), answer and advance (reply),
   or re-engage (gone quiet). One objective per email.
3. **Proof available**: only facts in the product knowledge or the
   prospect's notes. If you don't have it, don't say it.
4. **One next step**: the single question or CTA.

## Three hook types (draft all three in your head, send the strongest)
- **A. Direct relevance**: name their situation. "Your practice's site has
  doctor bios but no video of the doctors."
- **B. Curiosity or contrast**: a credible tension. "Most firms in Broward
  post every week. The ones getting calls post the same three answers on
  video."
- **C. Story or evidence**: a real observation or a real fact about Klip.
  "We have a podcast room ten minutes from your office that most realtors
  in Fort Lauderdale don't know exists."
Pick by audience: owners and doctors respond to A, marketers to B, creators
and realtors to C. Never exaggerate to make a hook work.

## Write for the speaker
- Sounds like Enrique talking, not a brochure. Read it aloud.
- Spoken words over written ones: "shoot", "film", "post", "book", not
  "leverage", "content solutions", "omnichannel".
- Specific nouns beat adjectives: "a 3-camera podcast set" beats "a
  state-of-the-art studio".

## Klip's message formulas
- **Lead reply** (someone asked about the studio): thank them, answer their
  exact question, add one helpful detail (price, room or availability from
  product knowledge), give one clear next step (booking link or a call
  time). Under 60 words when they wrote briefly.
- **Re-engage a quiet lead** (3 touches): day 1 something useful (an idea
  for their content), day 3 a simple question, day 7 an easy yes/no ("Should
  I close the loop on this?").
- **Cold sequence angle per email**:
  1. Observation about their content or presence + one question. No pitch.
  2. One relevant Klip fact (a room, the Content Day, 24h delivery) tied to
     what you observed + one question.
  3. Short, polite close-the-loop.

## CTAs that fit Klip
- Cold: a question they can answer in one line ("Who handles video for the
  practice right now?").
- Warm: "Want me to send a couple of times for a 15-minute call?" or the
  booking page when they're ready to book a room.
- Spanish: "Te mando un par de horarios para una llamada de 15 minutos?"

## Subject lines
Lowercase, 2-5 words, specific, honest. Good: "video for the practice",
"your podcast idea", "headshots for the team", "listing videos".
Never: fake "re:", "quick question", clickbait the email doesn't pay off.

## Repurposing angle (a strong Klip idea for email 2)
One studio session becomes a month of content: one podcast or 4-hour
Content Day -> clips, reels, quote cards, a newsletter blurb, a LinkedIn
post. Use it when the prospect posts rarely or inconsistently.
